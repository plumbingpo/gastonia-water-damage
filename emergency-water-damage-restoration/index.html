When to Call for Emergency Water Damage Restoration in Gastonia

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Part of your ceiling bows downward because water is pooling on top of it.
  • Wet carpet, wood flooring or wallboard has stayed saturated longer than a few hours.
  • A growing pool on the floor is heading for the next room or running downstairs.
  • An outlet, a light fixture or the ceiling above has water dripping out of it.
  • Water is still flowing and you cannot find or reach the shutoff valve.
  • Nobody was home when it started, so the length of the leak is unknown.
  • Gallons keep spilling from a ruptured water heater tank.

Emergency Water Damage Restoration, Step by Step

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle emergency water damage restoration from start to finish:

1

Answer the Call

A person answers at any hour, asks about the source, the rooms affected and any safety concerns, and tells you what to do while a crew is on the way to your home.

2

Shut Off the Water

Once on site, the crew looks for electrical and contamination risks before closing off or isolating the supply behind the leak. Until the flow ends, no other step does much good.

3

Remove Standing Water

We pull standing water with pumps and truck-mounted extractors, move furniture out of wet areas, and map hidden moisture in walls and floors with meters and thermal imaging.

4

Stabilize and Plan

The crew does not go until the drying equipment, meaning air movers and dehumidifiers, is running. Afterward our team explains what turned up, what to expect during the 3 to 5 days of drying ahead, and which records your insurance company needs.

Maintenance Tips for Emergency Water Damage Restoration

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Know where your electrical panel is so power to wet rooms can be cut safely.
  • Pipes running through unheated areas need insulation to keep them from freezing and bursting.
  • Before anything goes wrong, store the phone number for an emergency restoration crew.
  • Shut off the water at the main whenever you leave home for more than a few days.
  • Locate the main shutoff valve now, and show each person at home how to turn it off.
  • In basements and other low areas, keep documents and valuables up on shelves.
