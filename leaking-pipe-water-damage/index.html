Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Gastonia

Most pipe leaks start without any warning. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. For Gastonia homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

The plumbing repair stops the water, but it does not remove what has already soaked in. This is where our team comes in. Our crew maps the damp materials with thermal imaging and moisture meters, opens up no more than necessary, and runs dehumidifiers and air movers until the readings return to normal. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stop the Water SupplyWhen a toilet or sink line is dripping, shut the valve on the supply right at that fixture. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Read the MeterShut off all taps and fixtures, then go look at the water meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Have the Area MeteredAsk for meter readings of the area before anyone patches it closed. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

Insurance treats slow leaks differently from sudden ones. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Read your policy and talk to your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Cold snaps that freeze piping in unheated spaces, leaving small cracks that leak after the thaw.
  • Shifting soil or abrasion wearing through water lines that run beneath a concrete slab.
  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
  • Tiny pinholes that form as corrosion gradually works through aging copper pipe.
  • Hidden joints, soldered or crimped, that fail behind walls and above ceilings.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Particleboard cabinet bases under a sink swell and crumble once they absorb water. Swollen board stays swollen, and after a lengthy leak the base often has to be replaced.

Flooring Damage

A leak in a wall or under the slab sends water beneath the floor covering. Hardwood cups, laminate swells at the seams, and adhesive under vinyl and tile lets go.

Ceiling and Drywall Damage

Brown water rings and blistered paint show up when a line drips behind a wall or over a ceiling. If the board keeps taking on water it loses its stiffness, starts to sag and may have to be removed.

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. During a slow leak, the back side of the drywall can be covered well before the painted face shows one spot.

Our Process

Our Leaking Pipe Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

Every wet area gets marked after a scan of the walls, cabinets and floors with meters and thermal imaging, and that includes places water followed the framing to, some distance from the leak.

2

Make Targeted Openings

To get at wet cavities we open only specific spots, take out unsalvageable cabinet parts, drywall or insulation, and seal anything moldy in bags so its spores stay out of the rest of the home.

3

Structural Drying

Drying equipment stays in place, typically for 3 to 5 days, until moisture in the framing and subfloor is back to normal. On every visit we take new readings and reposition equipment as things dry out.

4

Treat and Document

We apply antimicrobial treatment to affected surfaces and give you moisture logs and photos. Those records show the area was dry before repairs and help with any claim.
